# Rewriting Techniques

Concrete moves to apply once AI-tells are flagged (see `ai-tells.md`). The goal each time: replace generic/average phrasing with something specific, opinionated, or textured.

## 1. Vary sentence rhythm

- After any 2–3 sentences of similar length, break the pattern. Follow a long sentence with a short one.
- Fragments are allowed for emphasis. "Not always. Sometimes it's the opposite."
- Read the paragraph and mark each sentence's rough length (short/medium/long) — if it's a flat pattern, break it up.

## 2. Trade abstraction for specificity

- "A variety of tools can help" → name 2–3 actual tools.
- "This can improve results significantly" → give an actual number, example, or scenario if one is available; if not, say what kind of improvement, not just that improvement happens.
- "Many businesses struggle with this" → describe one concrete situation instead of the vague plural.

## 3. Cut the hedge stack

- Pick the strongest true claim the source material supports and state it directly.
- "This may potentially help improve performance in some cases" → "This improves performance when X" or "This can help — most noticeably when X."
- Reserve hedging for places genuine uncertainty exists, not as a reflexive tic on every sentence.

## 4. Break symmetric/listy structures

- If every idea is being forced into a 3-item list, break one out into its own paragraph or fold two together.
- Vary list format: sometimes a sentence with commas, sometimes a bulleted list, sometimes just narrative prose — don't default to bullets for everything.

## 5. Give it a point of view

- Human writing usually has an opinion, even a mild one. "Some people prefer X, others Y" is safer but flatter than "X is the better default for most people, though Y makes sense if..."
- If the source material supports a recommendation, make it — don't just present balanced options when a lean is warranted.

## 6. Fix openings and closings specifically

- **Opening**: replace a generic topic restatement with a claim, a specific scenario, a surprising fact, or a direct address to the reader's actual situation.
  - Generic: "In today's competitive market, businesses need effective marketing strategies."
  - Better: "Most small business Facebook pages post the same three things — a product photo, a discount, a holiday greeting — and wonder why engagement is flat."
- **Closing**: end on something specific — a concrete next step, a pointed observation, or a genuine open question — not a summary restating the whole piece.

## 7. Tone-matching

Match register to context rather than defaulting to a neutral "helpful assistant" tone:

- **Casual/social (e.g. Facebook post, local ad)**: contractions, shorter sentences, direct address ("you"), occasional informal punctuation.
- **Professional/client-facing (e.g. proposal, report)**: fuller sentences, fewer contractions, still avoid the AI-tell vocabulary list — professional doesn't mean generic.
- **Technical/SEO content**: keep clarity and keyword relevance, but still vary sentence structure and cut filler — technical content is one of the most common places AI-tells cluster because "comprehensive coverage" gets mistaken for value.
- If unsure which register fits, ask the user rather than guessing — tone mismatches are the fastest way for a rewrite to feel wrong even if each individual sentence is fine.

## 8. Sanity check before returning

- Every factual claim in the original is still present and unchanged.
- Nothing was added that wasn't implied by the source (no invented statistics, examples, or quotes).
- Read it once more — if it still sounds like it could be the top Google result for the topic with no distinguishing voice, another pass is needed.
