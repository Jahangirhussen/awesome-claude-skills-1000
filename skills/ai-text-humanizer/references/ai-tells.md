# Common AI Writing "Tells"

Patterns that make text read as AI-generated. Flag these on first pass, then rewrite around them — don't just delete the word, restructure the sentence.

## Overused transition/hedge words

Words fine in small doses but flagged when they appear repeatedly or open multiple paragraphs:

- "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Additionally," "In addition,"
- "It's important to note that..." / "It's worth noting that..."
- "In today's fast-paced world..." / "In today's digital age..."
- "When it comes to X..."
- "Navigate the complexities of..."
- "Unlock the potential of..." / "Harness the power of..."
- "Delve into..." / "Dive deep into..."
- "In conclusion," / "To sum up,"
- "Whether you're X or Y, ..." (false-choice framing used to sound inclusive)

## Structural patterns

- **Rule-of-three overuse** — every list is exactly 3 items, every sentence has exactly 3 adjectives. Vary it: sometimes 2, sometimes 5, sometimes just 1 strong word.
- **Symmetric sentence pairs** — "It's not just about X, it's about Y." Fine once in a whole piece; repeated, it's an obvious tell.
- **Generic opening** — restating the topic instead of starting with a claim, question, or specific scenario.
- **Generic closing** — a paragraph that just re-summarizes the piece instead of ending on a specific point, call-to-action, or open question.
- **Uniform sentence length** — most sentences land in the same 15–22 word range. Real writing swings between short fragments and longer, winding sentences.
- **Over-hedged claims** — "may potentially help improve," stacking multiple hedge words where one would do, or where a direct claim would be stronger.

## Vocabulary tells

- Overuse of: "robust," "seamless," "leverage," "streamline," "holistic," "cutting-edge," "game-changer," "elevate," "unparalleled," "tapestry," "testament to"
- Abstract nouns standing in for concrete detail: "a variety of factors," "numerous benefits," "several considerations" — replace with the actual factors/benefits/considerations.

## Tone tells

- Relentless positivity/enthusiasm with no texture — every point framed as exciting or beneficial, no acknowledgment of tradeoffs, limits, or nuance.
- Overly balanced hedging on things that don't need it ("there are pros and cons to consider") when a direct opinion or recommendation would serve the reader better.

## What to do instead

For every flagged pattern, ask: *what would a person who actually knows this topic say here instead?* That person would usually give a specific example, state an opinion, or just say the thing plainly instead of hedging around it.
